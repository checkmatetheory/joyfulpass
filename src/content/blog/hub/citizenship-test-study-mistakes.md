Preparing for a citizenship or settlement test gets a lot easier once you know which habits quietly waste your time. These seven come up again and again in study plans that leave people feeling busy but not actually ready.

## 1. Starting with random online quizzes

Quizzes have their place, but not when you can't tell whether their answers are current or tied to the official source. Read the official guide first, then use practice questions to test your recall against it.

## 2. Studying only the easy topics

It's natural to drift back to material you already know — it feels productive. Instead, track your wrong answers and spend the extra time on your weakest chapters, however unappealing they are.

## 3. Memorising letter choices

Question order and wording change. Learn *why* an answer is right, not just that it was "C" last time, or you'll be lost the moment the options move.

## 4. Skipping timed mock tests

Knowing a fact on your sofa is not the same as recalling it against the clock. Timed practice gives you an honest picture of how ready you really are.

## 5. Waiting until the booking is close

Short, regular sessions stick far better than a frantic final week. Give yourself enough runway to circle back to the topics that don't come easily.

## 6. Treating a single pass as proof you're ready

One good practice score can be luck. Aim for a consistent margin above the official pass mark before you book or attend.

## 7. Ignoring official updates

Rules, fees, and test-day procedures all change. Check the official government guidance whenever you're booking or submitting an application — not just once at the start.
