The Life in the UK Test is not a test of your personal immigration history. It checks knowledge from the official handbook, *Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents*. A focused study plan is more effective than trying to memorise random online questions.

## What is on the Life in the UK Test?

You answer 24 multiple-choice questions in 45 minutes. To pass, you need 18 correct answers. Questions are based on the official handbook and cover the UK's history, values, laws, government, and everyday society.

For the latest details on format and eligibility, use [GOV.UK's Life in the UK Test guidance](https://www.gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test). For an overview of the exam structure, read our guide to the [Life in the UK Test format](/life-in-the-uk-test/blog/life-in-the-uk-test-format/).

## Topics that deserve extra revision

People often find factual recall harder than broad concepts. Build extra practice around:

- **British history:** key events, monarchs, wars, and cultural developments
- **Government and law:** Parliament, elections, devolved governments, and civic responsibilities
- **UK geography:** countries, capital cities, landmarks, and national symbols
- **Society and traditions:** public holidays, sport, arts, religion, and community life

Do not skip a chapter because it feels less relevant to your daily life. The real test can draw questions from across the handbook.

## A four-week study plan

Your ideal timeline depends on your starting point, but four weeks works well for many learners.

### Week 1: Learn the structure

Read through the handbook topics and take a short practice test. The goal is not a high score yet; it is finding your unfamiliar areas.

### Week 2: Study weak chapters

Choose one or two weak topics each day. Read the relevant material, then answer practice questions immediately afterwards. Reviewing an explanation while the question is fresh helps the information stick.

### Week 3: Add timed mock tests

Start taking full 24-question tests under a 45-minute limit. Review every wrong answer, including questions you guessed correctly.

### Week 4: Build consistency

Repeat mixed mock tests until you are regularly scoring above 75%, ideally with a margin. Consistent results are more useful than one unusually high score.

## Use practice questions carefully

Free question banks can be useful, but they may contain outdated facts or invented questions. Check that your study material follows the current official handbook.
