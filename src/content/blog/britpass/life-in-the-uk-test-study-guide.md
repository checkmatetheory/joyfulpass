The Life in the UK Test isn't a test of your personal immigration history — it checks knowledge from the official handbook, *Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents*. A focused plan built around that book beats memorising random online questions every time.

## What's on the Life in the UK Test?

You answer 24 multiple-choice questions in 45 minutes, and you need 18 right to pass. Every question comes from the official handbook and covers the UK's history, values, laws, government, and everyday society.

For the latest on format and eligibility, go straight to [GOV.UK's Life in the UK Test guidance](https://www.gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test). For a walk-through of the exam structure, see our guide to the [Life in the UK Test format](/britpass/life-in-the-uk-test/blog/life-in-the-uk-test-format/).

## Topics that deserve extra revision

In practice, it's the specific factual recall that trips people up, not the broad concepts. Put extra reps into:

- **British history:** key events, monarchs, wars, and cultural developments
- **Government and law:** Parliament, elections, devolved governments, and civic responsibilities
- **UK geography:** countries, capital cities, landmarks, and national symbols
- **Society and traditions:** public holidays, sport, arts, religion, and community life

Don't skip a chapter just because it feels distant from your daily life — the real test pulls questions from right across the handbook, and the dates and names are exactly where people lose easy marks.

## A four-week study plan

Your ideal timeline depends on where you're starting from, but four weeks suits most people.

### Week 1: Learn the structure

Read through the handbook topics and take a short practice test. Don't chase a high score yet — the point is to surface the areas you're shaky on.

### Week 2: Study weak chapters

Pick one or two weak topics a day. Read the material, then answer practice questions straight afterwards. Reviewing the explanation while the question's still fresh is what makes it stick.

### Week 3: Add timed mock tests

Start sitting full 24-question tests under the 45-minute limit. Review every wrong answer — and the ones you guessed right, too.

### Week 4: Build consistency

Keep taking mixed mocks until you're regularly clearing 75% with room to spare. A steady run of solid scores tells you far more than one lucky high one.

## Use practice questions carefully

Free question banks can help, but plenty carry outdated facts or invented questions. Check that whatever you're using tracks the current official handbook.
