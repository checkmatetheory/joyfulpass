The single number every candidate wants confirmed before booking is the pass mark. For the Life in the UK Test it's **75%** — and here's exactly what that translates to on the day.

## The pass mark in plain numbers

- The test has **24 questions**.
- You need **18 correct** to pass.
- That's **75%**, so you can miss up to **6 questions** and still walk out with a pass.
- You have **45 minutes**, which is roughly 1 minute 50 seconds per question — plenty of time.

There's no partial credit and no negative marking. Each question is either right or wrong, and your final score is simply the count of correct answers out of 24.

## Do you find out your score?

You won't be given a percentage breakdown. At an approved test centre you're told whether you passed or not shortly after finishing — the result is pass/fail, not a detailed report card. That's another reason to aim well above 18 rather than hoping you scraped it.

## Why "just passing" is the wrong target

Six wrong answers sounds like a comfortable buffer, but three things quietly eat into it on test day:

1. **Nerves.** Even well-prepared candidates misread a question or two under pressure.
2. **Trick wording.** Some questions hinge on a single word — "must" versus "can", a date, a name.
3. **Topic luck.** You don't choose which parts of the handbook come up, so a weak chapter can cost you several questions at once.

Candidates who pass first time almost always score **above 90% on their mock tests** in the days before booking. That cushion is what turns a nervy attempt into a routine one.

## How to build a safe margin

- Take **full 24-question mock tests** under a timer, not casual quizzes.
- Track which chapters you miss most and revise those specifically.
- Don't book the real test until you're clearing **22 out of 24** consistently.

[BritPass](/britpass/) mock tests match the real 24-question, 45-minute, 75%-to-pass format exactly, so the number you see in practice is the number that counts on the day.

## Before you book

Always confirm current fees and requirements at the official source: [GOV.UK — Life in the UK Test](https://www.gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test). Then keep practising until 18 out of 24 feels like your floor, not your ceiling.
