*Discover Canada: The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship* is the official study guide for the Canadian citizenship test. It contains a lot of information, but you do not need to absorb it all in one sitting.

## Start with the official guide

Download or read the current [Discover Canada guide from the Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/migration/ircc/english/pdf/pub/discover.pdf). This should be your primary source. Practice apps and quizzes work best when they reinforce the guide rather than replace it.

## A three-week Discover Canada study plan

### Week 1: Build a foundation

Read the guide from beginning to end without trying to memorise every date. Notice the broad themes: Canada's regions, its history, how government works, and the rights and responsibilities of citizens.

At the end of each reading session, answer a short set of questions. Mark topics that feel unfamiliar.

### Week 2: Work through weak areas

Divide the guide into topics and spend each session on one area. For example, study government and elections one day, then geography and regional history the next.

Use active recall: close the guide and explain what you remember before checking your notes. This is more effective than rereading a paragraph several times.

### Week 3: Practise under timed conditions

The citizenship test has 20 questions and a 30-minute limit. Start taking full practice tests without notes. Review every error and return to the relevant section of *Discover Canada*.

Aim to score above 15 out of 20 consistently. Repeated good results are a better readiness signal than one high score.

## Avoid common study mistakes

- **Using only random web quizzes:** some are outdated or poorly sourced
- **Skipping geography:** provinces, territories, and regions can appear on the test
- **Memorising answers without context:** question wording can change
- **Waiting until test week:** short, regular sessions give you more time to retain facts
