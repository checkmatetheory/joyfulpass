Knowing the Canadian citizenship test format makes it easier to set a useful study target. The test checks your knowledge of Canada and the rights and responsibilities of citizenship. It is based on the official *Discover Canada* study guide.

## What is the pass mark for the Canadian citizenship test?

The written test has 20 multiple-choice and true-or-false questions. You have 30 minutes to complete it, and you need at least 15 correct answers to pass — a 75% pass mark.

The current format and eligibility rules are set by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC). Confirm the details in [IRCC's citizenship test guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/become-canadian-citizen/citizenship-test.html), especially if your notice to appear says something different.

## What topics are on the test?

Questions can cover the content in *Discover Canada*, including:

- **Canadian history** and major events
- **Geography**, provinces, territories, and regions
- **Government and elections**, including how Parliament works
- **Rights, freedoms, and responsibilities** of citizens
- **National symbols, economy, and cultural life**

The questions do not all focus on one chapter. A good score needs broad coverage, not just strong knowledge of Canadian history.

## How to study for a passing score

Start by reading the current [Discover Canada guide](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/migration/ircc/english/pdf/pub/discover.pdf). Then use practice questions to identify gaps in your recall.

Try this approach:

1. Read one section of the guide at a time.
2. Answer practice questions on that topic without looking at notes.
3. Review explanations for every incorrect answer.
4. Take mixed, timed practice tests after each few sections.

Aim to score comfortably above 15 out of 20 in several different mock tests. A single passing score can happen through luck; repeated results show you understand the material.

## Do you need to memorise every detail?

You do need a solid grasp of factual information, but memorising isolated lists is rarely the fastest route. Connect facts to the wider story of Canada: how provinces fit together, why institutions exist, and how rights are balanced by responsibilities.
