A failed citizenship test is deflating, but it doesn't automatically end your application — and in the files I worked on, that fear was almost always worse than the reality. IRCC normally gives eligible applicants another chance to meet the knowledge requirement. Your exact next step depends on your file and the instructions you're sent.

## What happens after an unsuccessful test?

IRCC tells you what comes next through your account, by email, or in another official message. Often, applicants who don't pass the first written test are invited to sit it again. If someone is unsuccessful a second time, IRCC may arrange an interview with a citizenship official instead of another written test.

Don't lean on a timeline you heard from a friend or read in a Facebook group. Processing times and arrangements vary, and your notice from IRCC is the only source of truth that matters.

## Can you take the Canadian citizenship test again?

Usually, yes. IRCC sets the process and timing for a second attempt, so read every message closely and keep your contact details current — a missed deadline is a self-inflicted setback you don't want.

Check the official [citizenship test information from IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/become-canadian-citizen/citizenship-test.html) for the current rules. For what an appointment actually feels like, see our guide to [Canadian citizenship test day](/canadapass/canadian-citizenship-test/blog/citizenship-test-day-tips/).

## How to prepare differently for a retest

Grinding through the same random quizzes again probably won't fix what went wrong. Treat the first attempt as a diagnosis instead:

- **Go back to Discover Canada.** Read the official guide itself, not just someone's summary of it.
- **Sort your weak topics.** Track whether history, government, geography, or the responsibilities of citizenship cause the most errors.
- **Practise under test conditions.** Do 20 questions in 30 minutes, no notes.
- **Review explanations.** Knowing *why* an answer is right beats memorising that it was "C."
- **Space out your revision.** Revisit the hard facts across several days rather than cramming them into one evening.

The pass mark is 15 out of 20. Aim to clear that comfortably in practice, so a couple of unfamiliar questions on the day don't sink you. Our overview of the [Canadian citizenship test pass mark and format](/canadapass/canadian-citizenship-test/blog/canadian-citizenship-test-pass-mark/) helps you set that target.

## Stay focused on the official process

The knowledge test is only one piece of a citizenship application — IRCC may still need to confirm other eligibility requirements. Keep copies of every official message and respond quickly whenever it asks for something.
