Knowing the mechanics of test day removes a surprising amount of anxiety.

## Before you arrive

Bring your notice to appear, valid identification matching the name on your application, and your PR card. IRCC's instructions on your notice take precedence over anything you read online — always check the letter you were sent.

## During the test

You'll get 20 questions and 30 minutes. Most people finish in well under 20 minutes. The test is written, not oral, for most applicants aged 18 to 54.

## If you don't pass

You're not out of options. IRCC typically invites you to a second attempt, and if that doesn't succeed, to an interview with a citizenship officer instead of a third written test. This isn't a reason to under-prepare, but it is a reason not to panic.

## After the test

If you pass the written test and meet the other requirements, you'll be scheduled for the citizenship ceremony, where you take the Oath of Citizenship. Check [IRCC's official guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/canadian-citizenship/become-canadian-citizen/citizenship-test.html) for current timelines, which vary by region and case volume.
