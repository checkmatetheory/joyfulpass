Not passing SERU first time is frustrating, but it isn't the end of your application — as long as you use your next attempt well.

## How many attempts you get

Private hire applicants currently get **two attempts** at the SERU assessment. If you don't pass within two, TfL cancels your licence application. You'd then have to start again — reapplying and paying the fees a second time — which costs far more than preparing properly for the first attempt.

At the time of writing, TfL charges **£40** for a first SERU attempt and **£30** for a re-sit. Always confirm current fees with TfL before you book.

## Work out why you didn't pass

Before you rebook, be honest about what went wrong:

- **Did you run out of time?** Often a sign of relying on the handbook too much. See our [open-book tips](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/seru-open-book-handbook-tips/).
- **Did one topic catch you out?** Safeguarding and equality scenarios trip up people who only memorised definitions.
- **Did the sentence-completion or reading questions feel unfamiliar?** Practise those formats specifically.

## Prepare differently for the re-sit

Don't just repeat the same questions. Go back through each topic in the [SERU study guide](/serupass/seru-assessment/study-guide/), then practise under timed conditions until you're comfortably above the 60% pass mark across several different sets. The [free SERU practice tests](/serupass/seru-assessment/practice/) are a good start; SeruPass Pro adds full-length timed mocks and saves every mistake so you can retry them.

Your re-sit is your last attempt before starting over, so book it only when you're consistently passing. Check [TfL's SERU guidance](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/taxis-and-private-hire/licensing/SERU-for-private-hire-drivers) for the current process. SeruPass is an independent study app.
