The honest answer: SERU isn't designed to trick you, but plenty of people underestimate it. It's very passable if you prepare — and surprisingly easy to fail if you don't.

## What makes SERU feel hard

- **The clock.** Around 36 questions in roughly an hour, and being open book tempts people to look things up. That's how they run out of time. See our [open-book tips](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/seru-open-book-handbook-tips/).
- **Scenario questions.** Many questions describe a situation — a distressed young passenger, a guide dog, an aggressive customer — and ask what you should do. Memorising definitions isn't enough; you need to understand the principle.
- **Mixed formats.** Sentence completion and reading questions test understanding and English, not just recognition.
- **Limited attempts.** Knowing you can't just keep retaking it adds pressure. Here's [what happens if you fail](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/seru-attempts-what-if-you-fail/).

## What makes it easier than it looks

- The pass mark is **60%**, which leaves room for a few mistakes.
- The topics are practical and sensible — most answers come down to safety, fairness and following the law.
- You know the format in advance, so nothing on the day needs to be a surprise.

## How to make it straightforward

Learn each topic properly, then practise under timed conditions until you're clearing 60% comfortably and consistently. Start with the [free SERU practice tests](/serupass/seru-assessment/practice/) and the [SERU study guide](/serupass/seru-assessment/study-guide/).
