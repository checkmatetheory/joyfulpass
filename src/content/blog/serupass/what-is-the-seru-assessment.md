If you're applying for a London private hire licence, you'll hear "SERU" a lot. It stands for **Safety, Equality and Regulatory Understanding**, and it's the assessment Transport for London (TfL) uses to check that new private hire drivers know the rules, the law and their responsibilities before they start carrying passengers.

## What the SERU assessment covers

SERU isn't a driving test. It's a knowledge assessment built around the things a professional driver in London has to get right:

- **Your obligations as a licensed driver** under the Private Hire Vehicles (London) Act 1998 and its regulations
- **TfL's policies and guidance** for private hire
- **Equality and disability** — including the Equality Act 2010, protected characteristics and assistance dogs
- **Safeguarding** — protecting children and adults at risk from harm and abuse
- **Passenger and driver safety**, and **road and vehicle safety**

Our [SERU study guide](/serupass/seru-assessment/study-guide/) walks through each of these topics in order.

## What the test looks like

The assessment is taken on a computer and has around 36 questions in a few formats: multiple choice, sentence completion, and a couple of reading-and-understanding questions. You need **60% to pass**, and you have roughly an hour. It's **open book** — you can refer to the Private Hire Driver's Handbook — but it's timed, so you can't look everything up. We cover the details in our guide to the [SERU pass mark and format](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/seru-pass-mark-and-format/).

## Where it fits in your licence application

SERU is one step in getting licensed, alongside the enhanced DBS check, a medical, the topographical assessment and TfL's English language requirement. See [how to get a PCO licence in London](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/how-to-get-a-pco-licence-in-london/) for the full sequence.

## How to get ready

The drivers who pass first time know the material well enough that the handbook is only a safety net.
