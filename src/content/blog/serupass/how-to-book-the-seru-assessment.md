You don't book SERU the way you'd book a driving theory test on day one. It comes as part of your licence application, once TfL is ready for you.

## When you can book

The order is:

1. **Apply** for your London private hire driver's licence through TfL, with your DBS check and medical under way.
2. **Wait for TfL's review.** Once your application has been reviewed, TfL contacts you and asks you to book your assessments — typically the topographical assessment, the SERU assessment and the English speaking and listening test (unless you're exempt).
3. **Book a slot** using the instructions TfL gives you.

Our guide on [how to get a PCO licence in London](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/how-to-get-a-pco-licence-in-london/) covers the whole application.

## What it costs

At the time of writing, the SERU assessment costs **£40** for your first attempt and **£30** for a re-sit. Fees change, so confirm the current amount when you book.

## On the day

- You take SERU **on a computer** at TfL's assessment centre.
- Bring the identification TfL asks for in your booking confirmation.
- Arrive early — being rushed costs you focus you'll need.
- It's **open book**, but timed: roughly an hour for around 36 questions. Here's [how to use the handbook without running out of time](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/seru-open-book-handbook-tips/).

## Book when you're ready, not before

You get limited attempts, so only book once you're clearing the 60% pass mark comfortably in practice. Start with the [free SERU practice tests](/serupass/seru-assessment/practice/).

Booking details and fees are set by [Transport for London](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/taxis-and-private-hire/licensing/SERU-for-private-hire-drivers). SeruPass is an independent study app and can't book assessments for you.
