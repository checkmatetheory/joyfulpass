To hold a London private hire licence you need to show you can communicate well in English. TfL sets this at the equivalent of **B1 level** — enough to talk to passengers, understand them, and read and follow the rules you work under.

## Two parts to the requirement

TfL's English language requirement is split in two:

1. **Speaking and listening** — a test of spoken English, taken with an approved provider, unless you have evidence TfL accepts that you already meet the standard.
2. **Reading and writing** — covered by the **SERU assessment**. That's why SERU includes sentence-completion and reading questions, not just multiple choice.

So even if you're exempt from the speaking and listening test, you still take SERU.

## What B1 means in practice

B1 is an intermediate level. At B1 you can handle everyday conversations, understand clear standard speech, and read straightforward texts on familiar topics — like a passenger's instructions, or a rule in the driver's handbook.

## Exemptions and evidence

TfL accepts certain evidence as proof that you meet the requirement, which can exempt you from the speaking and listening test. The accepted evidence and any disability-related exemptions are set by TfL, so check the current list on the [TfL English language requirement page](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/taxis-and-private-hire/english-language-requirement) before you book anything.
