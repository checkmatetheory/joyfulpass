SERU rewards steady preparation far more than a last-minute cram. Here's a plan that fits around shifts and other commitments.

## Week 1: learn the material, topic by topic

Work through one topic area a day, reading the notes and then answering questions straight away while it's fresh:

1. **Passenger, road and vehicle safety** — seat belts, phones, fatigue, safe drop-offs.
2. **Safeguarding** — recognising when a child or adult may be at risk, and reporting it.
3. **Equality and disability** — the Equality Act 2010, protected characteristics, assistance dogs.
4. **Regulatory understanding** — how private hire is licensed in London and what that means for you.
5. **Dealing with passengers** — complaints, lost property, and staying professional when things get difficult.

Our [SERU study guide](/serupass/seru-assessment/study-guide/) and [topic practice](/serupass/seru-assessment/topics/) follow this exact order.

## Week 2: practise like the real thing

Now switch to mixed practice under time pressure:

- Do full-length sets against the clock, not just a few questions at a time.
- Practise **sentence completion** and **reading** questions — they catch out people who only drilled multiple choice.
- Review every mistake and read why the right answer is right.

Try the [free SERU practice tests](/serupass/seru-assessment/practice/) to start. SeruPass Pro adds full 36-question timed mocks and keeps a history of your mistakes so you can retry them until they stick.

## Know when you're ready

You're ready to book when you're clearing 60% comfortably across several different practice sets. Remember you only get limited attempts before your application is affected — see [what happens if you fail SERU](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/seru-attempts-what-if-you-fail/) — so it pays to be genuinely ready rather than hopeful.
