"PCO licence" is the everyday name for a London private hire driver's licence, issued by Transport for London (TfL). Here's the route from application to licence.

## 1. Check you're eligible

Before you start, make sure you meet TfL's basic requirements, which include being old enough, having the right to work in the UK, and having held a full driving licence for long enough. The exact criteria are on [TfL's licensing pages](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/taxis-and-private-hire/), so check them first.

## 2. Get your enhanced DBS check

You need an enhanced DBS (criminal record) check through TfL's approved route, and TfL expects you to sign up to the DBS Update Service.

## 3. Pass a medical

You must meet the medical standard TfL requires (the DVLA's Group 2 standard) and have a doctor complete TfL's medical form.

## 4. Apply to TfL

Submit your application online and pay the application fee.

## 5. Take your assessments

Once TfL has reviewed your application, it invites you to book your assessments:

- **SERU** — safety, equality and regulatory understanding. Start with [what the SERU assessment is](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/what-is-the-seru-assessment/).
- **Topographical** — map reading and route planning.
- **English speaking and listening** — unless you're exempt. See [TfL's English language requirement](/serupass/seru-assessment/blog/tfl-english-language-requirement/).

## 6. Wait for TfL's decision

TfL checks your documents and results before making a decision. The whole process commonly takes a few months, so plan your finances and working arrangements around it.

## The step you control most

You can't speed up TfL's review, but you can make sure you pass SERU first time. Use the [free SERU practice tests](/serupass/seru-assessment/practice/) and the [SERU questions and answers](/serupass/seru-assessment/questions/) to get ready before your invitation arrives.
