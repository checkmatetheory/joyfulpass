Most adults applying for German naturalisation (Einbürgerung) need to show knowledge of Germany's legal and social order — and the Einbürgerungstest is the usual way to prove it. But not everyone has to sit it, and it's worth checking where you stand before you book anything.

## Who normally takes the test?

If you're applying to naturalise as an adult, you'll generally be asked to pass the Einbürgerungstest as one part of the application. It sits alongside the other requirements — things like your period of lawful residence, a secure livelihood, German language ability (usually B1), and a commitment to the free democratic basic order.

The test itself only covers that knowledge requirement. Meeting it doesn't tick every other box, and the naturalisation authority (Einbürgerungsbehörde) still assesses the rest of your file.

## Who may be exempt?

In my experience the exemptions surprise people — several students who dreaded the test turned out not to need it. You may not have to take it if:

- You hold a **German school-leaving qualification** (Hauptschulabschluss or higher), which can satisfy the knowledge requirement on its own.
- You **can't take it because of illness, disability, or age**, backed by appropriate evidence.
- You're **under 16**, as children are generally naturalised on different terms.

None of these is automatic. The naturalisation authority decides based on your evidence, so ask them directly rather than assuming.

## The "Leben in Deutschland" route

If you've done an integration course, you may have taken the **"Leben in Deutschland"** test — it draws on the same question catalogue, and a passing result is normally accepted for naturalisation. Our guide on [Leben in Deutschland vs the Einbürgerungstest](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/blog/leben-in-deutschland-vs-einbuergerungstest/) explains how the two relate, so you don't sit the same test twice by accident.

## Confirm before you commit

Naturalisation rules have changed in recent years, and the details depend on your circumstances and your Bundesland. Check the current requirements with [BAMF](https://www.bamf.de/EN/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/Einbuergerung/einbuergerung-node.html) and your local Einbürgerungsbehörde before you plan around the test.
