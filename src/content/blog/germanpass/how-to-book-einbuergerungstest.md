The Einbürgerungstest is a required step for most people applying for German citizenship. Here's how booking actually works.

## Step 1: Find an approved test centre

You don't take the test at a government office. It's run by **approved centres** — most commonly a **Volkshochschule (VHS)**, but also some other adult-education providers. Look for a centre near you that offers the *Einbürgerungstest* (not only the "Leben in Deutschland" test, though they use the same questions).

## Step 2: Register and pay the fee

You register directly with the centre for a test date. There is a **fee** (commonly around €25 — confirm the current amount with your centre). You'll usually need to bring valid photo ID on the day.

## Step 3: Prepare with the official catalogue

Because the whole question catalogue is public, preparation is straightforward:

- Learn the **300 general questions** by theme.
- Revise the **10 questions for your federal state**.
- Sit full **33-question, 60-minute** mock tests until you're comfortably above the 17-mark pass line.

[GermanPass](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/) mock tests mirror the real format, so the test itself feels like one more practice run.

## Step 4: Sit the test and collect your certificate

You answer 33 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes. Pass, and you receive a **certificate** — this is what you submit as part of your naturalisation application to your local **Einbürgerungsbehörde**.

## Always use the official source

Fees, eligibility and the naturalisation process can change and vary by region. Confirm everything before you act: [BAMF — Einbürgerungstest](https://www.bamf.de/EN/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/Einbuergerung/einbuergerung-node.html). Then start working through full mock tests so you're ready on the day.
