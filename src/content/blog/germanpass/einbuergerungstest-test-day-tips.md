Almost everyone I teach is more nervous about the room than the questions. That's usually backwards — by test day you've seen every question in the catalogue, so the goal is simply to not let the setting rattle you out of marks you already know.

## Where the test happens

You'll sit the Einbürgerungstest at an approved test centre — often a Volkshochschule (VHS) or another BAMF-authorised provider. You book a slot in advance and pay a small fee (around €25 at the time of writing; confirm the current amount when you book). Our guide on [how to book the Einbürgerungstest](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/blog/how-to-book-einbuergerungstest/) walks through that step.

## What to bring

Keep it simple, and check your invitation for anything centre-specific:

- **Valid photo ID** — your passport or residence document, matching your booking
- **Your invitation or confirmation**, if the centre issued one
- **Nothing you don't need** — phones and notes go away during the test

Arrive early. A relaxed ten minutes beforehand beats a stressed sprint through the door.

## How the paper works

You get **33 multiple-choice questions and 60 minutes** — 30 general questions plus 3 for your Bundesland. You mark your answers on an answer sheet, one option per question. You need **17 correct** to pass.

That's roughly two minutes a question, which is plenty. My advice from watching hundreds of sittings: answer the ones you're sure of first, then circle back to anything you paused on. And read the questions with "not" or "forbidden" in them twice — those single words flip the correct answer and are where confident people slip.

## Getting your result

You won't get your score the moment you finish. The result and your certificate come afterwards — typically a few weeks later, depending on the centre. If you pass, keep the certificate safe; you'll need it for your naturalisation application. If you don't, it's not the end of the road — here's [what happens if you fail](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/blog/einbuergerungstest-what-if-you-fail/), and you can retake it.

## The best test-day prep is earlier

Nothing settles nerves like knowing the material cold. Run full timed mocks in the days before so the format feels routine — [GermanPass](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/) mirrors the real 33-question, 60-minute test.
