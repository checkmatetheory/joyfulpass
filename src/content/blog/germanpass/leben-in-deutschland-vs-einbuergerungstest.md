If you've started researching German citizenship, you've probably seen two names — the **"Leben in Deutschland" test** and the **Einbürgerungstest** — and wondered which one you actually need. The short answer: they're almost the same test, but taken for different reasons.

## They use the same questions

Both tests draw from the **same official catalogue** — 300 general questions plus 10 per federal state — and both have the same format: **33 questions, 17 to pass, 60 minutes**. If you've prepared for one, you've prepared for the other.

## The difference is the purpose

- **Leben in Deutschland** is usually taken at the **end of an integration course** (Integrationskurs). It certifies your knowledge of German society as part of that course.
- The **Einbürgerungstest** is taken specifically as a step toward **naturalisation** (becoming a German citizen).

Because the content is identical, a "Leben in Deutschland" certificate is generally accepted for naturalisation too — but if you're not doing an integration course, you simply register for the Einbürgerungstest directly.

## Which should you take?

- Doing an **integration course**? You'll take *Leben in Deutschland* at the end, and that certificate normally counts for citizenship.
- **Not** doing a course, or just need the certificate for naturalisation? Register for the **Einbürgerungstest** at an approved centre such as a Volkshochschule.

Either way, the preparation is the same. [GermanPass](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/) mock tests mirror the shared 33-question format, so you're ready for whichever one you sit.

## Confirm the details

Rules can vary by region and change over time. Always check the official source before you decide: [BAMF — Einbürgerungstest](https://www.bamf.de/EN/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/Einbuergerung/einbuergerung-node.html).
