The single number every candidate wants confirmed before booking is the pass mark. For the Einbürgerungstest it's **17 out of 33** — and here's exactly what that translates to on the day.

## The pass mark in plain numbers

- The test has **33 questions**.
- You need **17 correct** to pass.
- You have **60 minutes**, which is nearly two minutes per question — plenty of time.
- There's no negative marking: each question is simply right or wrong.

So you can get up to **16 questions wrong** and still pass. That sounds generous, but there are good reasons not to aim for the bare minimum.

## Why "just passing" is the wrong target

Three things quietly eat into that buffer on test day:

1. **Nerves.** Even well-prepared candidates misread a question or two under pressure.
2. **The state questions.** 3 of the 33 are specific to your Bundesland — easy marks if you've revised them, lost marks if you haven't.
3. **Similar-sounding options.** Some questions hinge on a single detail — a date, an institution, a right.

Candidates who pass comfortably usually score **well into the high 20s** on mock tests before booking.

## How to build a safe margin

- Take **full 33-question mock tests** under the 60-minute limit, not casual quizzes.
- Revise the **3 state-specific questions** for your Bundesland — they're published in the catalogue.
- Don't book until you're clearing the **mid-to-high 20s** consistently.

[GermanPass](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/) practice tests match the real format exactly, so the score you see in practice is the score that counts on the day.

## Do you find out your score?

You're told whether you passed, and passing earns you a certificate you submit with your naturalisation application. Always confirm current requirements at the official source: [BAMF — Einbürgerungstest](https://www.bamf.de/EN/Themen/Integration/ZugewanderteTeilnehmende/Einbuergerung/einbuergerung-node.html).
