Good news first: the Einbürgerungstest is one of the cheapest steps on the way to German citizenship.

## The test fee

Taking the Einbürgerungstest costs **€25**, and the fee is the same across Germany. You pay it when you register with the test centre — often a Volkshochschule (VHS) or another approved provider. Our guide on [how to book the Einbürgerungstest](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/blog/how-to-book-einbuergerungstest/) covers registration.

## Retakes

If you don't pass, you can take the test again as often as you need — but each attempt is a new booking with the fee paid again. See [what happens if you fail](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/blog/einbuergerungstest-what-if-you-fail/).

## The naturalisation fee is separate

The €25 covers only the test. Your application for naturalisation (Einbürgerung) has its own fee, paid to your local naturalisation authority. At the time of writing that's **€255 for an adult**, with a reduced fee for children naturalised with a parent. Check the current amount with your Einbürgerungsbehörde.

## Your certificate

When you pass, you receive a certificate — usually by post a few weeks after the test. It doesn't expire, so there's no need to retake it if your application takes a while. More in our guide to [Einbürgerungstest results](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/blog/einbuergerungstest-results/).

Make one booking count: practise with the [free Einbürgerungstest practice tests](/germanpass/einbuergerungstest/practice/) first. Fees are set by the authorities and can change. GermanPass is an independent study app.
